# Effects library

The Effects button in Figma Design's toolbar, after Actions, lists effects Ligma makes right in the file (`src-tauri/src/effects.rs`). This is how they're built.

The library opens with a search field, which matches effects' names and descriptions, then a grid of tiles, four to a row, each a picture of its effect over its name. Arrow keys move through the tiles and typing anywhere searches. The pictures are made by the effects themselves, the first time the library opens, on samples drawn to show each best: a sunset for the dithers and Bitcrush, rings for Chromatic Aberration, checks for Fisheye and a pawn on a floor or on water for Shadow and Reflection. A picture effect's `thumb` names its sample, backdrop and settings. Room's left under the search for category tabs.

## How effects work

- **No plugins.** Ligma's page script runs in Figma's page, where Figma's console plugin API, `window.figma`, works on the open file. Effects call it directly, so there's no plugin window, no plugin sandbox, no network and no paywall. The API exists only in files that can be edited, so the button only shows there.
- **Live in the file.** What an effect makes is real layers on the canvas from the first change, updated once a frame as you drag a field. Cancel removes them. Apply calls `figma.commitUndo()` once, so the whole result is one undo step.
- **Figma's own controls.** Panels and menus are drawn from Figma's CSS variables (`--color-bg`, `--color-bg-secondary`, `--color-border`, `--color-bg-menu`, `--elevation-500`, `--radius-large` and so on), which Figma sets on the page's body for the light or dark theme. They use its type (Inter, 11px), its dropdowns, segmented controls and menus, and its brand blue on sliders and the main button, so they sit with Figma's own panels rather than looking like a plugin's.
- **Adding one.** Each effect is an entry in `EFFECTS` (`id`, `name`, `about`, `icon`, `open`). `open(figma)` gets the plugin API and the selection, and builds its panel from the shared controls: `select`, `segments`, sections and the footer, as Repeater's does with its labeled sliders.
- **Editable results.** An effect can keep what it made in a group tagged with shared plugin data (`ligma` namespace), with its settings, so selecting the group and opening the effect again changes it instead of starting over.

## Repeater (built)

Repeats the selected layer, live. Its panel has a preview of the whole result up top, drawn from a picture of the layer, so it shows even when the copies are off screen; preset chips; Repeat as; labeled sliders, each with its value beside it, where distances and turns ease out from zero so small values get most of the slider, and End size has 100% in the middle; values can be typed in past the slider's ends, dragged left and right as Figma's fields are, or stepped with the arrow keys (Shift by 10); Reset; and Place, which says how many copies it places. The preview is grouped from the start, so the layers panel shows one group, and Cancel puts the original back where it was (not in auto layout, where a group would join the flow):

- **Trail:** each copy a step right and down from the last, turned by a set angle, and curving: the step's direction turns each time, so trails bend into arcs, and each step is as much smaller or larger as the copies are, so shrinking copies spiral inward.
- **Radial:** around a ring through the original, over a sweep of up to 360°, turning the copies to follow the ring or not.
- **Grid:** rows and columns, with gaps.
- **To the last copy:** size, opacity and a blend toward a color change from the first copy to the last, evenly or easing in, out or both.
- **Presets:** Trail, Fade out, Spiral, Arc, Ring, Fan, Grid and Echo.
- **Result:** copies stacked under or over the original. With Keep editable (on by default) the original and its copies are grouped as a Repeater: select it and open Repeater to change it, and the copies are made again from the original as it is then, so they follow changes to it. Without it, the copies are plain layers.
- **Limits:** up to 400 copies. Copies in auto layout are placed absolutely. Sections can't be repeated.

## Disintegrate (built)

Breaks the selected layer into pieces and throws them, live, as After Effects' Shatter does, with the same controls and model:

- **Shape:** Pattern (Glass, Cells, Squares, Triangles, Bricks, Planks or Hexagons) and how many Pieces, counted over the part of the layer that isn't transparent. Glass, as AE's, is the layer split again and again by straight lines at random angles, the largest piece each time, so cracks run on across pieces and they come out as long, sharp slivers; Cells is irregular Voronoi cells, each cut again by a line or two. Every piece is a polygon with straight edges. Shuffle changes the pattern.
- **Forces:** one or two, as AE's Force 1 and Force 2: Position, Depth, Radius and Strength. A force is a sphere, Depth behind the layer and Radius across, in percent of the layer's larger side. The pieces inside it fly away from its middle, fastest at the middle and slowing to nothing at its edge, so those in the middle mostly come at the camera, growing as they near it, while those toward the edge barely move; the damage stays on the subject unless Strength is high. Negative Strength pulls in. Each force shows in the preview as a dot and a dashed ring where its sphere meets the layer: drag the dot to move it, or the ring to change its radius.
- **Time,** in seconds, as AE's comp time.
- **Physics,** as AE's: Rotation speed (turns a second), Tumble axis (Free tumbles in 3D, a piece thinning as it turns edge-on, though never quite flat, as AE's have thickness; Spin only turns in the layer's plane; None), Randomness (which also gives each piece a velocity of its own in any 3D direction, so some go against the force and gravity, back over what's still whole, as AE's do), Viscosity, Mass variance, and Gravity with its direction, in tenths of the layer's size a second, as speed is. A camera as far from the layer as about twice its size gives the depth.
- **Snap:** the Thanos snap as it's made in AE, with Shatter, tiny pieces and a gradient wipe: the same physics, with each piece only breaking once a ragged wipe (Raggedness, Edge softness) spreading from each force over Spread time reaches it, a little force, and gravity as a wind. Pieces then fade, shrink and lighten.
- **Drawing:** the layer is exported as a picture (up to 2× and 4,096 pixels across); the pieces that moved are cut out of it and each drawn where it's gone with the canvas, farthest from the camera first, transformed and clipped to its polygon, so edges stay smooth and sharp. The preview draws at once; the image in the file follows a moment after, and Disintegrate makes it at full size.
- **Presets:** Dissolve (the default) and Wipe, matched against Thanos snaps made with AE's Shatter, a figure's side dissolving into dust that lifts away and a wipe sweeping across the top of a phone mockup; Snap and Dust; Glass (AE's defaults), Corner (a corner breaking off, matched against AE's Shatter on an app icon), Crack, Explode and Bricks. Picking a mode starts from its first preset.
- **Result:** an image of what's drawn, over the original. With Keep editable (on by default) the original is kept under it, hidden, in a Disintegrate group: select it and open Disintegrate to change it, made again from the original. Without it, only the image is left.

## Bitcrush (built)

Pixelates, posterizes, dithers and glitches the selected layer: Figma's "Bitcrush lab" shader effect (its `main.ts` and `features.json`), its WGSL ported line for line to run on the layer's picture in the page, in workers so Figma keeps up while a large one's crushed, as the plugin API can't make Figma's shader effects. Every control is the shader's, with its ranges and defaults: Pixel size and aspect, Color bit depth, Color mode (RGB or Grayscale), Dither pattern (Off, Ordered Bayer or Noise), strength and scale, Glitch block size, displacement and amount, RGB split distance and angle, Noise, Contrast, Saturation, Mix, and a random seed (Shuffle). Sizes are in points, as the shader's are, so the look holds however large the picture. Presets are Classic (the shader's defaults), Game Boy, 8-bit, Glitch and Dither. The result is an image the layer's size over the original, kept, hidden, in an editable Bitcrush group, as Disintegrate's is.

## Picture effects (built)

Dither, Halftone, Modulate, Chromatic Aberration, Fisheye, Liquify, Displace, Motion Blur, Glow, Shadow, Reflection and Rim Light each make a new picture from the layer's, as Bitcrush does, and share its workings: the layer read as a picture, a small one at once for the panel's preview and the file while it's changed, then up to 2× and 4,096 pixels across for applying; the pixel work in a worker, so Figma keeps up; and an image over the original, kept, hidden, in an editable group named after the effect. Each is an entry made by `pictureEffect` with its defaults, presets, panel and a pixel function, which takes the picture, the settings and its pixels a point and gives a picture and where it sits, so Shadow and Reflection can draw past the layer. Sizes are in points, so the look holds at any size. Nothing's generated: every result is worked out from the layer's own pixels.

- **Dither:** Dither Boy's error diffusion (Floyd–Steinberg, Atkinson, Jarvis–Judice–Ninke, Stucki, Burkes, Sierra, Two-row Sierra and Sierra Lite, serpentine or not), ordered (Bayer 2×2, 4×4 and 8×8 and clustered dots), random and threshold dithers, on cells of a pixel size, with a strength, in black and white, two colors, grays, handheld green, sepia, cyan and magenta, or posterized color, after brightness, contrast and gamma. Presets are Classic, Atkinson, Bayer, Handheld, Newsprint, Retro color and CGA.
- **Halftone:** a print screen of dots, lines, squares, diamonds or crosses on a grid turned to an angle, each as large as the layer is dark there, in one ink or in CMYK, each ink on its own angle, with dot size, softness, ink and paper, and paper shown or left clear.
- **Modulate:** Dither Boy's modulation dithers: lines that thicken where the layer's dark, waves that swing wider there, or rings around a center.
- **Chromatic Aberration:** red, green and blue drawn apart, out from a center and more toward the edges as a lens does (Falloff), or all one way, as red and blue, red and cyan, green and magenta, or a smooth spectrum.
- **Fisheye:** a fisheye's bulge, or a pinch, as a lens maps angles to a flat picture, within a radius around a center, its edge kept where it is, with Zoom and the lens's circle cut out or not.
- **Liquify:** Photoshop's distort filters. Twirl turns it more toward the center, Pinch squeezes it in or puffs it out, Ripple sends rings out from the center as on a pond and Zigzag twists them back and forth, all within a radius around a center, and Wave moves it across waves running at an angle. Presets are Twirl, Whirlpool, Pinch, Puff, Pond, Zigzag and Wave.
- **Displace:** each pixel taken from a little way off, as a map says: smooth noise or turbulence's sharper ridges (scale, detail, stretch, which way it moves and a seed), or the layer's own tones, light parts pushed one way along an angle and dark the other. Split moves red and blue more and less than green, for a prism's fringes. Presets are Wobble, Heat haze, Turbulence, Smear and Prism.
- **Motion Blur:** smeared along a line (Linear, with a distance and angle), out from a center (Zoom) or around it (Spin), both ways or as a Trail behind its motion. Each pixel averages eight samples along the path, then eight between those and eight between those again, so long blurs stay smooth. Presets are Pan, Speed, Shake, Zoom burst and Spin.
- **Glow:** light spilling past its brightest parts (Bloom, with a threshold and softness) or past its whole shape (Outer), blurred at three sizes and added over it as light is, so it washes toward white where it's strongest, with a radius, intensity and tint. Presets are Bloom, Dreamy, Highlights, Neon and Halo; Glow only leaves the layer out.

Liquify, Displace, Motion Blur, Glow and Rim Light keep a photo, a picture with nothing see-through, to its bounds, its edge pixels repeated where they reach past it, and grow a cutout to fit what they draw past its edge.

- **Shadow:** for a cutout of a figure or object standing at its lowest solid row (Ground moves that line). Cast lays its silhouette on the ground away from the light, each point as far from its footing as it's high times Length, toward Direction, foreshortened by the ground's Perspective, and spreading with Spread as a near light's does. It's sharp where it touches and softer and fainter toward its end, with a contact shadow, the footing's outline squashed flat and blurred, under it. Drop is a flat drop shadow. Presets are Afternoon, Noon, Evening, Backlit, Studio, Floor and Drop; Shadow only leaves the layer out.
- **Reflection:** the cutout mirrored at its lowest solid row onto a floor or water, fading (Fade) and blurring as it goes from the footing, with Fresnel to make it fainter toward the viewer as a real surface's is, Streaks to smear it downward as wet ground and water do, and Gap, Squash and Skew for a surface seen at an angle. On Water, waves in perspective, smaller toward the footing and larger toward the viewer, shift it up and down and side to side by about their Height, their steepest faces breaking it apart (Broken) and catching Glints, with a seed to Shuffle. A tint colors it, and Surface lays that color under it as a pool around the footing. Presets are Glossy, Mirror, Satin, Wet ground, Still water, Lake, Choppy and Angled.
- **Rim Light:** a light behind a cutout catching its edges on the side facing Direction, found by looking Width toward the light and seeing how much of the cutout's there, with softness, intensity and a color. Shade darkens the rest as a backlit subject is, and Halo lets the light spill past its edge. Presets are Golden hour, Cool edge, Backlit, Neon and Dramatic.

Effects stack. With an effect's group selected, or a layer inside it, Effects lists Edit for that effect first, then every effect to apply on top of it, which wraps the group in its own; each stays editable on its own. Updating an effect under others makes those above again from it, each with its saved settings.

With a layer that has Ligma effects selected, Figma's right panel shows a Ligma Effects section under its own Effects, laid out as Figma's (a 32-pixel title row, 24-pixel cards with a 1-pixel inset border, the eye on hover in place of the card's end, and −), but apart from them, listing them with the last applied on top, each with a summary of its settings. A row opens its effect to change; the eye hides or shows it, showing what it was made from, and for one under others makes those above again from it; dragging a row by its grip reorders the stack and − removes one, both by making the stack again from the original, each effect with its saved settings, under the old one, which stays until the new one's done, so nothing flashes; + adds one on top. Stacked groups are named after the original, `Effect · name`.
